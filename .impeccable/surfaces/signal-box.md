# Surface brief: Signal Box (games/signal-box/play.html)

Mode: Experience. The diagram and the lever frame are the whole screen; menus are the line register between puzzles.

## Direction contract

THESIS: The inside of a railway signal box, not a transit map app. The puzzle is an enamel track diagram hung on the wall, the controls are a real lever frame on the floor, and trains show up as train describer berths with amber headcodes. It refuses the defaults of the genre: no neon route lines on black, no glowing tiles, no cartoon trains.

OWN-WORLD: Vitreous enamel white (#eef0ea) for the diagram, printed in near-black ink (#17211d) with signal red (#c62f2a) arms. The room around it is box-green (#1f332c) above a dark wood floor (#2b211c). Levers are painted by job: red for signals, black for points, blue (#1f4d99) for the single-line direction levers, each with a steel handle and a brass number plate. Occupied track lights amber (#f2a33a); the train describer berths are black with amber lettering. Each of the five lines has its own station nameboard colour, as the old railway regions did: maroon, dark blue, green, chocolate and tangerine.

STORY: The player opens the box, picks a line and a station, and works the frame. Each puzzle is a track diagram, a lever frame and a timetable: set the points, clear the signals, and bring every train to its platform on time without two trains meeting. The five lines each bring one new idea: points and signals, the interlocked frame, the single line and its direction lever, the yard and shunting, and the timetable. Stars go to the fewest lever pulls.

FIRST VIEWPORT: The title is a station nameboard over a working diagram: a timetable plays itself out behind it, levers moving in the frame below as trains cross the board.

FORM: Pinned by the user's choice of the Signal Box concept (enamel track diagram plus a row of red, blue and black levers), which beats the roll. Seed key 9905421c, assigned index 3 not built because of the pin. Raises kept from the dealt challengers: cassette deck fascia (every function gets its own labeled control: each lever carries its number plate and a drawn glyph for what it works), sign shop working drawing (times and sequences are written onto the drawing itself: the timetable reads like working notes, clock times in a fixed-width face), night instrument six-pack (few readouts, each trusted at a glance: the clock, the pulls count, the par).

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance
