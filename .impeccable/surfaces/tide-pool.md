# Surface brief: Tide Pool (games/tide-pool/play.html)

Mode: Experience with a light Operate layer. The pool is the thing being looked at; the journal beside it is where the player reads and decides.

## Direction contract

THESIS: A naturalist's watercolor field journal, not a cute aquarium sim. The pool is painted as washes on wet sand, with pigment pooling at its edges, and the journal beside it reads like a field notebook: plain ink labels, Latin names in italic, ticks for what has been observed. It refuses the casual default of candy gradients, bouncing coins, star ratings and timers that nag.

OWN-WORLD: Wet sand (#d8c7a0), a violet-grey rock shelf in layered washes, sea teal in the pool (#5fb1c9 at the edges to #1f6f8f in the deep), a navy ink (#173a5e) for every line of text, a pale journal page (#fafcfb), and one coral (#e8553f, #c8402c on filled buttons) for the one action per screen, the selection ring and the tide needle. Kelp green only on the "happy" and "observed" marks. A workhorse sans for the journal, an italic book serif only for Latin names.

STORY: The player shapes a rock pool with stones, weed, sand and shells; each high tide brings visitors that stay only if the pool gives them a home, food and safety; low water leaves shells on the sand and the chance to watch someone do something worth noting. The guide fills page by page, new coves open, and spring tides at new and full moon bring the rare ones.

FIRST VIEWPORT: The painted pool fills the view area with the sea along its top and the channel the tide comes through; the journal (bottom on portrait, right on landscape) opens with the tide clock (moon, one day's tide curve, a coral needle for now), four tabs, and the residents list with how each one is doing.

FORM: Pinned by the user's choice of the Tide Pool concept (a naturalist's watercolor field journal), which beats the roll. Seed key a3a52c96, assigned index 6 not built because of the pin. Raises kept from the dealt challengers: daylight section (the tide moves across the whole scene as a band, and the tide clock names the state of the water the way the day was named), seven-segment display (absence is designed too: undiscovered guide entries are drawn as ghost silhouettes, with three empty observation pips), HyperCard stack (the guide is a stack of entries you open and close in place), leather skeuomorph (progress is a row of filled pips, not a bar), sleeve collage (the selected creature is ringed in coral in the pool and its row is washed in the journal).

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance
