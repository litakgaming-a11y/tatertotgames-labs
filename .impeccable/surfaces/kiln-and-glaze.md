# Surface brief: Kiln & Glaze (games/kiln-and-glaze/play.html)

Mode: Experience with an Operate layer. The pots and the glazes are what the player looks at; the studio card beside them is where they decide.

## Direction contract

THESIS: A working pottery studio at night, not a cosy craft sticker book. The scene is a slate-grey studio wall with a wheel, a kiln and a pegboard of test tiles, so the only saturated colour anywhere is the glaze itself. The side panel is a stack of index cards: recipes written as a potter writes them, oxide by oxide. It refuses the casual default of pastel bubbles, star ratings, timers and coin fountains.

OWN-WORLD: Slate studio wall (#353c40 to #262b2e), wet clay greys and buffs, the pale grey index card (#f4f5f2) with faint blue ruling, ink (#1e2a31), one cobalt (#2f55c0) for the action on each screen and the selection ring, and kiln-ember amber only for heat: the glow in the kiln and the temperature trace. Everything else that has colour is a fired glaze.

STORY: The player throws a pot by pulling its walls on the wheel, mixes a glaze from a base and seven oxides, loads the kiln, chooses how hot it goes, how starved of air it is and how fast it cools, then opens the door to see what came out. Named glazes fill a board of 36 test tiles; townspeople order pots by form and colour; reputation brings a gas kiln, porcelain, new oxides and finally a wood kiln.

FIRST VIEWPORT: The wheel with a lump of clay on it fills the scene; the card panel (bottom on portrait, right on landscape) shows coins and reputation, five station tabs (Wheel, Glaze, Kiln, Shelf, Book) and the clay choice with one cobalt button to start.

FORM: Pinned by the user's choice of the Kiln & Glaze concept (a potter's test-tile board and handwritten recipe cards), which beats the roll. Seed key 4fb507cf, assigned index 5 not built because of the pin. Raises kept from the dealt challengers: glazier partition (the Book is a grid of panes where unknown glazes are frosted and a found one fills with its colour; nothing slides, the kiln reveal is a slow brightening), oscilloscope bench (the firing is drawn live as a temperature trace on a graticule with the air in the kiln as a second, dimmer channel), silkscreen (a glaze is built one pass at a time, one oxide per row, and the card shows each pass), depot blind (a firing that went wrong is shown plainly as a flaw on the pot, never as a failure screen).

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance
