# Design System: Signal Box

## Overview

Signal Box is the inside of a railway signal box. The puzzle hangs on the wall as an enamel track diagram: vitreous white enamel with the track printed in near-black, signals drawn as a post and a red semaphore arm, and the lever number beside everything a lever works. Below it, on a dark wood floor, stands the lever frame: one painted lever per job, each with a polished steel handle and a brass number plate carrying a small drawn glyph for what it works. Trains appear on the diagram as train describer berths, small black boxes with an amber headcode, and the track they stand on lights amber.

The room around the diagram is box green. Menus are the same room: the line register is an enamel card of stations, and every station name sits on a nameboard in the colour of its line, the way the old railway regions painted theirs.

## Colors

### Signal and lever colours
- **Signal red** (`#c62f2a`, pressed `#a32521`): signal levers, semaphore arms, and the one action on each screen (Open the box, Start, Next station, Try again).
- **Points black** (`#1c1f1e`): points levers.
- **Direction blue** (`#1f4d99`): the single-line direction levers and the blue rule drawn under a single line, with its arrow showing which way it runs.
- **Clear green** (`#2f8f58`): only the lamp on a signal that is off.

### Light
- **Amber** (`#f2a33a`): occupied track, train describer lettering, the clock digits, and focus rings. Amber means a train is there, or time is passing.

### Enamel and ink
- **Enamel** `#eef0ea`, shade `#dfe3dc`, hover `#f7f8f4`, rule `#c5cbc3`; **ink** `#17211d`, dim `#4d5a55`, faint `#7d8782`.

### The room
- **Room** `#1f332c`, deep `#172722`, edge `#0e1714`; **floor** `#2b211c`, frame bed `#1a1411`, slot `#0b0807`. Text on the room is `#d9e2dc`, `#c9d3cd` and `#e3ebe6`.

### Metal
- **Steel** `#c7ccce` with shade `#8c9396`, the lock block `#3a4042`, and **brass** `#b8963f` with brass ink `#2a2413` for the number plates.

### Line nameboards
Branch maroon `#7b2334`, Frame blue `#1f3f74`, Single Line green `#1e5b3c`, Yard chocolate `#5d3a24`, Timetable tangerine `#b4521a`. They appear only on nameboards and the tab chips of the line register.

### Named Rules
- **A colour is a job.** Red for signals, black for points, blue for direction, amber for trains and time. Nothing is coloured for decoration.
- **Route set is white light.** A road that is signalled or still held by a train shows a white core down the middle of the black track, as an illuminated diagram does.

## Typography

Three system stacks, because the project loads nothing from the network. Nameboards, headings, buttons and station names use Gill Sans where the device has it, the old railway face, and fall back to Segoe UI or the platform sans. Reading text is the system sans. Clock times, headcodes, times in the timetable and results are set in the platform's fixed-width face, because they are data read like a working timetable.

## Layout

Portrait stacks the box top to bottom: a top bar with Back, the station nameboard and sound; the diagram; the station note; the timetable; the clock and controls; and the lever frame across the bottom (24% of the height, 118 to 210px). Landscape puts the diagram on the left with the note, timetable and controls in a column on the right (34% of the width, at least 260px), and the frame runs along the bottom (25% of the height, 92 to 176px). The diagram scales to the largest cell size that fits, up to 54px a cell.

## Elevation & Depth

The enamel plate casts one soft shadow onto the wall. Everything else is flat: panels are an ink rule on enamel over a 62% green scrim, and depth in the frame comes from paint, a lit left edge on each lever and a darker lower band on each steel handle.

## Shapes

6px corners on controls and station rows, 8px on panels, nameboards and the enamel plate, 3px on berths and chips, 5px on timetable rows. Nameboards carry a white inner border and an ink outline, as a real enamel sign does.

## Components

### Enamel diagram (signature)
Track in ink, platforms as a pale enamel strip under the track with the station name below, buffer stops at dead ends, a stop board drawn as a small plate on a post. Points show their unset leg with a gap at the blade. A signal is a post with a red arm, level at danger and raised when off, with a lamp that turns green; shunt signals are a small disc with a red bar that turns 45 degrees. The lever number sits beside each signal and each set of points.

### Lever frame (signature)
One lever per job, painted by colour, with a steel handle and catch rod. A pulled lever stands shorter, leaning towards you. On an interlocked frame, a lever the frame will not release carries a steel block over its slot, and tapping it shakes the lever and says why.

### Train describer berth
A black box with the headcode in amber and an amber bar on its leading end.

### Timetable
One enamel row per train: headcode berth, where it is going and where it calls, what it is doing now, and the due time or the time it came in.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- Keep every colour to its job.
- Explain every locked lever in one plain sentence.
- Write times as a working timetable does.

### Don't:
- Don't use neon route lines, glows, or dark transit-map styling.
- Don't draw cartoon trains; trains are describer berths.
- Don't use emoji or glyph icons; the step, speed, restart and sound icons are drawn SVG, and everything on the diagram and frame is drawn on the canvas.

The one repeating-gradient texture is the diagonal hatching on disabled controls and locked stations, used the same way across the TaterTot games.
