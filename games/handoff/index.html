🔁 Handoff

The order is the game

You never touch the fight. You decide the order before it starts — and when one of yours falls, it hands something to whoever walks in next: a charged hit, a speed step, a shield, or nothing at all if the pairing is wrong. Order stops being about who meets the lead and becomes a sequence of three deaths you plan in advance.

Why it exists: Kinfold already measured the premise. Its own K-045 found stage 5-1 losing 0/10 in one squad order and averaging 64% across all three — ordering is worth more than levels, stars or runes, and the game resolves it on a menu screen. This moves that decision to the front of the fight and gives it a chain to sit in.

▶ PLAY NOW Runs in your browser · no install · works on phones

How to play

  1. 1Read the enemy squad. The one marked LEADS meets your first slot.
  2. 2Drag your three into order. Each slot shows its verdict against the enemy it will actually meet.
  3. 3Read THE CHAIN: what each of yours hands to the next when it falls.
  4. 4Lock in and watch. There is no input during the fight at all.

The question, and the measurement

Does order swing outcomes enough to carry a game, and do handoffs add to that or just decorate it? The probe runs all six orderings across many seeds, then runs the best ordering with handoffs on and off.

What it would cost Kinfold

K-003 survives literally — there is no input during the battle. The real cost is SKIP: a mandatory pre-fight puzzle on every grind re-clear is exactly the friction SKIP exists to remove. A per-stage memory that re-applies your last order must ship with the feature, not after it.
