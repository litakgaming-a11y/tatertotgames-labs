🔥 Crucible

Stop before it cracks

Feed cores into the pot one at a time. Each one raises the tier and raises the heat, and the pot tempers the whole batch — every core becomes a charm at whatever tier you reach. Past the line, each core you add might crack it. Bank early and you leave value on the table; push too far and the crack banks the last tier you passed.

Why it exists: Kinfold has no energy system by decree, which makes capture charms the one thing the entire free loop bottlenecks on — and they currently come from tapping a recipe. This attaches a toy to the actual scarcity, shaped so there is no downside limb: you cannot lose what you put in, only fail to gain more.

How to play

  1. 1FEED a core. The tier climbs, and so does the heat.
  2. 2Watch the line. Below it a feed can never crack; above it the crack chance is shown and rising.
  3. 3BANK to keep the whole batch at its current tier.
  4. 4If it cracks you still keep the batch at the last tier you passed. Nothing is ever confiscated.

The question, and the measurement

Would a player rather play this than tap the recipe it replaces? The probe simulates 500 shifts of a fixed 40-core budget under three policies and compares them to the flat tier-1 recipe.

What it would cost Kinfold

Nothing ratified — it is purely additive. But the first build of this probe failed its own EV gate at 10.05 against the recipe's 12.00, because Kinfold prices higher charm tiers to be per-core worse on purpose (“higher tiers are not a better deal, they are insurance”). Under that curve any toy that pushes toward higher tiers is EV-negative by construction. Batching is the fix, and it is the part that would need a decision. The other risk is tonal: this is a second variance system in a game that already has a gacha.
