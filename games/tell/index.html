🔶 Tell

Read the wind-up

Kinfold's battles fight themselves — the capture throw is the only thing you touch. Tell adds one more thing: every enemy attack telegraphs its element for about half a second before it lands, and you tap the element that beats it. Get it right and your Kinmon interposes, takes far less damage, and charges the capture meter.

Why it exists: the rule it questions (K-003) names its own risk out loud — “a battle you touch zero times is a screensaver and players skip it by day three”. Tell is built so that tapping wrong, or never tapping at all, resolves exactly as today's battle does. That optionality is the entire proposal, so the probe measures it rather than asserting it.

How to play

  1. 1Watch. The battle resolves on its own, as Kinfold's does.
  2. 2When the enemy winds up, a coloured glyph appears and a ring closes — that is your window.
  3. 3Tap the element that beats it. Ember beats Verdant, Verdant beats Tide, Tide beats Ember.
  4. 4A grey glyph means Radiant or Umbral: there is no answer to that one, which is the type chart teaching itself.

The question, and the measurement

Is Tell genuinely additive, or does it quietly become mandatory? The probe runs the same ladder under two policies — never answering, and always answering correctly — over 60 seeded runs each.

What it would cost Kinfold

It breaks K-003, the load-bearing rule of Kinfold's combat design — the throw would no longer be the only input. The defence is that it is one tap on one of three fixed pips, entirely optional, and teaches itself in a stage. But there is a sharper danger: Kinfold unlocks SKIP after a stage's first clear so that grinding is never labour. The moment anyone balances a stage around Tell, SKIP dies and this proposal loses its only real defence.
